# Exploratory Data Analysis Report
**Dataset:** `global_book_market_intelligence_2026.csv` · **Full analysis:** `notebooks/01_eda.ipynb`

---

## 1. What is this dataset?

14,828 catalog edition-records spanning 8 genres (Mystery, Fiction, History,
Romance, Biography, Self-Help, Children, Science Fiction — each represented
by roughly 1,350–2,000 records, i.e. a **near-evenly-sampled, curated**
catalog rather than an organic web scrape). Publication dates range from
1469 to 2025, heavily weighted toward 1970–2010. 85.5% of records report
English as the primary language.

## 2. The central finding: "rated" conflates two different questions

The raw data lets you ask two very different questions under one column:

- **"Was this book ever rated at all?"** (reach / engagement — a market
  question)
- **"How well was it rated?"** (quality — a content question)

Only the first has recoverable signal in this dataset:

| Question | Correlation / R² with metadata | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Does `rating_score` correlate with `pages`, `edition_count`, `author_count`, `language_count`? | \|r\| < 0.07 for every feature | No usable linear signal |
| Can a Random Forest predict `rating_score` from full metadata (rated subset, n=4,717)? | R² = 0.008 (near zero) on held-out data | Confirmed: no usable nonlinear signal either |
| Does `is_rated` (engagement) differ by genre, edition count, language reach? | Up to 5× spread by genre; ~2× by translation reach | **Strong, learnable signal** |

**Why:** quality is a property of a book's *content* (writing, story, cover,
marketing) — none of which this dataset captures. Reach/engagement, by
contrast, is substantially explained by *distribution* facts the dataset
does capture (how many editions, how many languages, which genre, which
publisher/author). This is why the project models `is_rated`, not
`rating_score` — see `reports/model_card.md` for the full modeling
treatment and `reports/data_quality_report.md` §3 for the related data-
quality issue in how "no rating" is encoded.

## 4. Catalog composition

- **Genre:** near-uniform by design (see §1) — this means genre-level
  engagement differences reflect real behavioral differences, not simple
  sampling artifacts of "there are more mystery books."
- **Language:** English (85.5%), German (1.7%), French (1.6%), Spanish
  (1.3%); everything else under 1% individually. Non-English predictions
  should be treated with more caution given the thinner data (see
  `reports/model_card.md`, limitations).
- **Time:** median first-publish year is in the 1990s; long thin tail of
  pre-1500 classical/foundational texts, individually verified as
  legitimate (`reports/data_quality_report.md` §4), not truncated.

## 5. Structural notes carried into modeling

- 826 repeated titles are legitimate distinct edition records, not
  duplicates — kept.
- Missingness in `pages` (11.5%), `primary_language` (5.1%),
  `first_publish_year` (0.9%), `primary_author` (2.3%), `primary_publisher`
  (1.4%) is handled with explicit categories/indicators, not silent
  imputation.
- The `is_rated` target is unified from two raw encodings of "no rating"
  (`NaN` and explicit `(0, 0)`) — see `reports/data_quality_report.md` §3.
